# Gym Progress Pro
## First-Session Onboarding Guide

**Goal:** Go from sign-in to a useful adaptive workout in about 10 minutes.

[Open Gym Progress Pro](https://gympro-agungajl.manus.space)

## 1. Sign in

Choose **Continue with Manus**. Your workouts, programs, custom exercises, and analytics are private to your account.

## 2. Build one repeatable split

Open **Splits** → **New split**.

Try this starter structure:

- `Upper A|Chest + back`
- `Lower A|Quads + hamstrings`
- `Upper B|Shoulders + arms`
- `Lower B|Posterior chain`

Open a day and add the exercises you actually plan to perform. Start with 2–4 movements per day. Set a rep range such as `8–12`, target sets such as `3`, and choose either a target RPE or target RIR.

## 3. Start your first workout

Choose **Log workout** or **Start this day**. Pick a mapped day, then enter:

- Weight
- Reps
- Either RPE or RIR
- Notes when something unusual happened

Do not enter both RPE and RIR for the same set. When finished, choose **Complete workout** so the session becomes part of your history and analytics.

While the workout is still in progress, you can edit the session name and notes, add another movement, edit or delete a target prescription, and edit or delete an incorrect set. Complete the workout only after the entries are clean; completed history is intentionally read-only.

## 4. Read your next target

Return to **Overview**. The recommendation card uses your completed performance and prescription to suggest an exact next target. Read the reason and any `AUTO-REG` note before your next session. The recommendation is advisory; you can manually change the load.

## 5. Create your first program block

Open **Programs** → **New program**.

Choose a name, goal, notes, and a duration between **4 and 12 weeks**. For each week:

1. Assign a split.
2. Set the intent, such as `Build volume` or `Peak strength`.
3. Keep normal weeks at `1.0` volume.
4. Mark a lighter week as **Deload** and use a lower volume multiplier such as `0.7`.
5. Save the week.

The week marked **NOW** is the current week. Choose **Start current week** to launch from its assigned split.

## 6. Use effort data consistently

Choose one effort language and stick with it:

- RPE 8 ≈ 2 reps in reserve
- RPE 9 ≈ 1 rep in reserve
- RPE 10 ≈ 0 reps in reserve

Or enter RIR directly from 0 to 6. Consistent estimates are more valuable than perfect estimates.

## 7. Review the signal once per week

Open **Analytics** and check:

- Weekly volume and hard sets
- Average intensity by muscle
- Volume change versus the comparison window
- Recovery heatmap
- Live e1RM cards for major lifts

Use the **7d** view for recent fatigue, **28d** for the normal overview, and **90d** for a broader trend. A volume spike or falling-volume warning is a prompt to think, not a command. Readiness is a training heuristic, not a medical assessment.

## 8. Keep your history portable

Open **History** when you need records outside the app:

- **Export CSV** for spreadsheets and analysis
- **Export PDF** and choose **Save as PDF** in the browser print dialog

## Your first-week checklist

- [ ] Sign in with Manus
- [ ] Create one split with at least two days
- [ ] Add prescriptions for your main movements
- [ ] Complete one workout with weight, reps, and RPE or RIR
- [ ] Try editing or deleting an active set before completing the workout
- [ ] Review the next-session recommendation
- [ ] Create a 4-week program and assign its first week
- [ ] Open Analytics after your second completed session
- [ ] Export History only if you need an offline copy

## Remember

Gym Progress Pro organizes the signal; you make the training decision. Review technique, fatigue, pain, sleep, and context before following any target. Completed workouts are preserved, and duplicating a program is the safest way to create a new variation.

# Gym Progress Pro
## Advanced Features User Manual

**Web application:** [gympro-agungajl.manus.space](https://gympro-agungajl.manus.space)  
**Audience:** Athletes, coaches, and program builders  
**Last updated:** November 2026

---

## 1. What Gym Progress Pro does

Gym Progress Pro is a private, adaptive workout log. It stores your workouts per account, turns completed sets into progression suggestions, tracks live strength markers, explains changes in training load, and lets you plan multi-week programs without rewriting completed history.

The app is organized around a simple loop:

1. **Plan** a split, exercise prescription, or multi-week program.
2. **Log** completed sets, including RPE or RIR when useful.
3. **Review** exact next-session targets, e1RM trends, volume, intensity, recovery signals, and history.
4. **Adjust** manually when the data or your day says the plan should change.

Recommendations are advisory. The app never silently changes a load you chose and does not provide medical advice.

## 2. Sign in and privacy

Open the web app and choose **Continue with Manus**. After authentication, your workout records, custom exercises, splits, programs, analytics, and exports are scoped to your signed-in account.

Use **Settings** to review your account and log out. Do not share an account if you need separate workout histories. Standard exercises are shared in the exercise library; custom exercises you create belong to your account.

## 3. Navigation at a glance

- **Overview** — weekly activity, recommendations, current split shortcuts, and recent history.
- **Splits** — reusable day-based training maps and exercise prescriptions.
- **Programs** — 4–12 week training blocks with weekly assignments and lifecycle controls.
- **Progress** — exercise-level load, volume, session trends, and recommendation cues.
- **Analytics** — volume, hard sets, intensity, recovery, heatmaps, warnings, and live strength markers.
- **History** — searchable completed sessions plus CSV and print-to-PDF exports.
- **Settings** — account details and logout.

On a phone, open the menu from the top-left button. The same routes and actions remain available at mobile widths.

## 4. First setup: exercises and splits

### Add a custom exercise

1. Open **Splits**.
2. Choose **Add custom exercise**.
3. Enter the movement name, muscle group, and equipment.
4. Save it. The movement becomes available in your private exercise library.

Use a clear name such as `Single-arm landmine press` rather than an abbreviation you may forget later.

### Create a split

1. Open **Splits** and choose **New split**.
2. Enter a name and short description.
3. Define one day per line using `Day name|Focus`, for example:
   - `Upper A|Chest + back`
   - `Lower A|Quads + hamstrings`
4. Save the split.

Open a day to add exercises. Each prescription can include:

- Target sets
- Target rep range
- Target RPE **or** target RIR
- Load increment
- Progression strategy: double progression, RPE autoregulation, or fixed load
- Rest-duration default

The app stores these as the starting prescription. When a workout begins, the active workout receives a snapshot so later program edits do not rewrite that workout’s targets.

### Edit or delete a split

On a saved split, choose the pencil control to edit its name or description. Each training day can also be edited to change its name or focus. Use the trash control to delete the entire split. Deleting a split removes its reusable map; it does not rewrite completed workout history because completed workouts keep their own prescription snapshots.

### Edit or delete a prescription

Inside an expanded training day, each exercise prescription has edit and delete controls. Edit target sets, target reps, target RPE/RIR, load increment, and progression strategy, then save. Delete removes the exercise from that reusable split day. The change affects future sessions, not completed sessions that already copied the prescription.

## 5. Logging a workout

### Start a session

Choose **Log workout** from the top bar, choose **Start workout** from Overview, or use **Start this day** from a split. Select a mapped day or start an open workout.

If the session was launched from a program week, the app keeps the originating week linked to the workout.

### Record a set

For each exercise, enter:

- Weight
- Reps
- RPE, from 0 to 10, **or** RIR, from 0 to 6
- Optional notes

Use either RPE or RIR for a set, not both. The logger displays an inline validation message before submission if both are entered, and the server validates the same rule.

A useful effort convention is:

- **RPE 10 / RIR 0:** no meaningful reps left
- **RPE 9 / RIR 1:** approximately one rep left
- **RPE 8 / RIR 2:** approximately two reps left

Effort values are estimates, not laboratory measurements. Use the same convention consistently so trends become more useful.

### Complete a session

Add any session notes, review the logged sets, and choose **Complete workout**. Completed sessions become available to Progress, Analytics, History, recommendations, and exports. Completed history is read-only from the history detail view.

### Edit, add, or delete an active workout

While a workout is **in progress**, you can:

- Edit the workout name and notes.
- Add another movement from the exercise library.
- Edit or delete a workout exercise’s target prescription.
- Edit a logged set’s weight, reps, RPE, or RIR.
- Delete an incorrect logged set.
- Delete the entire in-progress workout and its unfinished entries.

These controls are available beside the relevant session, exercise, and set rows. A completed workout does not expose these mutation controls. This boundary keeps the history used by recommendations and analytics stable; if a completed session is wrong, record the correction as a note or log a new session rather than rewriting the historical record.

## 6. Progressive overload recommendations

The recommendation cards on Overview and Progress use recent completed performance, prescription settings, rep-range completion, effort, load increments, progression strategy, and recent fatigue signals.

A recommendation can include:

- Exact target weight
- Rep range
- Number of sets
- Confidence level
- Evidence from recent sessions
- A readable reason
- An auto-regulation adjustment

Examples:

- `82.5 kg × 8–10 for 3 sets`
- Hold the load and add a clean rep next time.
- Reduce the target because recent effort was substantially above target.

Treat the card as a starting point. Review warm-ups, technique, pain, sleep, and the day’s readiness before accepting any target.

### Progression strategies

**Double progression** keeps the load stable while you move through the rep range, then increases load after the prescribed work clears the range and effort threshold.

**Fixed-load progression** favors adding reps while holding the current weight.

**RPE autoregulation** uses the logged effort signal to suggest a manual increase, hold, reduction, or set-count adjustment.

The engine can suggest a deload or fatigue reduction, but it does not alter your logged data automatically.

## 7. RPE and RIR auto-regulation

RPE and RIR are two ways to describe effort:

- **RPE** estimates how hard the set felt on a 0–10 scale.
- **RIR** estimates how many good reps remained on a 0–6 scale.

Gym Progress Pro normalizes either value into a shared effort signal while retaining the original metric. It can detect:

- Zero-reserve or unusually high-effort work
- Effort below the target
- Performance decline
- Repeated fatigue
- Top-of-range completion

The resulting adjustment is labeled in the recommendation card, for example `AUTO-REG / reduce load`. The label is advisory and marked for review before logging. You remain in control of the next load, reps, and sets.

## 8. Live 1RM estimations and strength markers

For loaded sets, Gym Progress Pro calculates an estimated one-rep max using the Epley formula:

`e1RM = weight × (1 + reps / 30)`

The app ignores zero-load or invalid loaded-set inputs for e1RM, so bodyweight work does not create misleading loaded strength markers.

### Where to find e1RM data

- **Progress** shows exercise-level trends, latest e1RM, best e1RM, and load context.
- **Analytics** shows compound-lift cards and live strength markers.

Major compound-lift buckets include squat, bench press, deadlift, and overhead press. Other loaded exercises can also produce valid e1RM data.

Use e1RM as a trend estimate, not a promise of actual maximal performance. Different rep ranges, technique, pauses, tempo, and fatigue can change the estimate.

## 9. Analytics dashboard

Open **Analytics** to combine training load, intensity, recovery, and strength signals.

### Filters

- **7d** — recent execution and short-term fatigue context
- **28d** — default training overview
- **90d** — longer trend context
- **Muscle lens** — all muscles or one muscle group

The selected filters update the dashboard’s charts, table, summaries, recovery map, and warnings together.

### Weekly volume and hard sets

The weekly chart shows volume load and hard sets. Volume load is calculated as:

`sets × reps × weight`

The comparison table includes weekly volume, hard sets, average e1RM, and change versus the previous displayed week. When a muscle lens is selected, the hard-set and average-intensity columns use that muscle’s values.

### Volume and intensity summaries

The muscle breakdown ranks volume by muscle group and shows hard sets, average e1RM, and volume bars. Use it to spot an underrepresented muscle group or a week where volume is concentrated.

### Volume signals and warnings

The dashboard can label the selected window as:

- **Volume spike** — volume is at least 30% above the comparison window.
- **Volume falling** — volume is at least 30% below the comparison window.
- **Volume steady** — change remains within the comparison band.
- **No volume signal** — there is not enough data for a useful baseline.

These are training signals, not medical conclusions. A spike may be intentional, and falling volume may be appropriate during a deload.

### Recovery heatmap and readiness

The heatmap shows completed-set stress by muscle and date. Darker cells mean more work landed on that muscle on that day.

Readiness scores use recent sets and time since the last session. The labels are explicitly **training heuristics**, not medical assessments. Combine them with your own recovery, technique, and symptoms.

### Live strength markers

Compound-lift cards show latest e1RM, all-time best e1RM, the percentage of best currently represented, and the best-set context. Use the cards to see whether load progression is supported by a broader strength trend.

## 10. Multi-week Programs

Programs turn a repeating split into a structured block.

### Create a program

1. Open **Programs**.
2. Choose **New program**.
3. Enter a name, goal, notes, and a duration from **4 to 12 weeks**.
4. Save the program.

The app creates ordered week definitions. The first week starts as a base week, middle weeks as build weeks, and the final week as a peak week.

### Configure each week

For every week, set:

- Label, such as Base, Build, Peak, or Deload
- Progression intent
- Assigned split
- Volume multiplier, such as `1.0` for normal volume or `0.7` for a lighter week
- Deload flag

Choose **Save week** after editing. Assigning a split makes its days available when you start that program week.

### Run the current week

The current week is marked **NOW**. Choose **Start current week**, select a day from the assigned split, and begin logging. The workout stores the originating block-week link and receives the split’s prescription snapshot.

### Advance, restart, and archive

- **Activate** moves a draft into an active program.
- **Advance week** moves the current-week indicator forward. The final week becomes completed.
- **Restart** returns a completed program to week 1 and marks it active.
- **Archive** removes a program from active planning without deleting its completed workout history.
- **Duplicate** creates a new user-owned copy with week settings copied, current week reset to 1, and status set to draft.

Edit or duplicate a program rather than rewriting the record of a completed workout. Historical sessions remain linked to the prescription source that generated them.

## 11. History and exports

Open **History** to search completed sessions and open a read-only detail view.

### CSV export

Choose **Export CSV** to download available completed workout history. The export includes session name, completion date, total sets, total volume, and notes. It is scoped to the signed-in user and does not modify records.

### PDF export

Choose **Export PDF** to open a print-optimized report. In the browser print dialog, select **Save as PDF**. The report uses the same workout-history data and is also scoped to your account.

## 12. Recommended operating rhythm

**Before training:** review the current program week, the recommendation card, and recent readiness signals.

**During training:** log weight, reps, and either RPE or RIR consistently. Add notes when a set was unusual.

**After training:** complete the workout so it contributes to recommendations and analytics.

**Weekly:** review weekly hard sets, volume change, average intensity, and muscle balance. Advance the program only when the block’s intent and your actual training support it.

**At the end of a block:** review Progress, Analytics, and History before duplicating or restarting a program.

## 13. Troubleshooting

**I cannot see colors or layout.** Refresh the deployed page and allow the stylesheet to load. The application includes a visible brand palette and responsive layout.

**My workout is not in Analytics.** Confirm the workout was completed, not left in progress. Analytics uses completed sets and completed workouts.

**The recommendation seems too aggressive.** Review the target effort, recent fatigue, and the auto-regulation explanation. Reduce or hold the load manually; the app never forces the recommendation.

**I selected RPE and RIR together.** Use one effort metric per set. Clear one field and submit again.

**My program week has no days.** Return to Programs, assign a split to that week, save it, then start the current week again.

**I need a different version of a program.** Use Duplicate, rename the copy, and edit the copy. The original and its historical sessions remain unchanged.

**I need to correct an active workout.** Use the pencil or trash controls on the session, exercise, or set row before completing it. Completed workouts are intentionally read-only.

## 14. Safety and interpretation notes

Gym Progress Pro is a training organization and tracking tool. Readiness, recovery, e1RM, volume warnings, and recommendations are estimates derived from the information you log. They are not medical advice or a diagnosis. Stop or modify training when pain, illness, unusual symptoms, or unsafe technique require it, and consult a qualified professional when appropriate.
